Corridor sites need 4 DC fast ports

Federal NEVI rules require AFC-serving DCFC stations to provide at least four network-connected DCFC ports capable of charging four EVs at once, with 97%+ average annual port uptime. That makes 2-DCFC concepts a local/community design unless a state explicitly treats them outside corridor buildout.

Source: FHWA NEVI final rule (2023); individual state NEVI plans.

The best gaps are not always the best businesses

Sparse western stretches create the strongest need, but low local EV density can make revenue fragile. High-traffic metros and college towns have better demand floors, but usually weaker gap scores. The screen therefore separates gap urgency from business durability.

Key thesis: prioritize sites where gap + amenity quality + grant path + utility cost can all be validated — not simply the longest dark stretch.

SCORING FRAMEWORK

Five factors.
One composite score.

01 Traffic density — AADT on the I-80 segment near the site. Higher traffic = more potential sessions. Scored 1 (under 5k/day) to 5 (over 40k/day).
02 DCFC gap — miles to the nearest open-network DC fast charger (50 kW+). Scored 1 (under 30 mi) to 5 (over 100 mi). Gaps create captive demand.
03 Local EV density — resident EV registrations within a 5-mile catchment. Drives non-highway utilization and membership revenue. Scored 1–5 by county EV penetration estimates.
04 Amenity anchor — quality of co-located services: lodging, full-service food, restrooms, retail. EV drivers dwell longer at quality stops. Scored 1 (bare highway pull-off) to 5 (full-service travel plaza).
05 Grant eligibility — NEVI corridor status, state RFP readiness, matching-fund practicality, and whether the site can meet corridor specs. §30C is not base-case after the June 30, 2026 placed-in-service deadline.
/25 Maximum composite score is 25. Sites scoring 17–25 are priority candidates. Sites below 13 are passed for this cycle.

Scores are preliminary estimates based on publicly available traffic, EV registration, NEVI program, and amenity data. They rank diligence priority, not investability or forecasted return. Human review required before using scores to commit capital.

The scoring model is a screening tool, not a financial projection. Each factor warrants independent diligence before site commitment.

THE CRITICAL GAPS

Four stretches where
EV options thin out.

CRITICAL — Laramie to Rock Springs, WY

~175 miles of I-80 where reliable open-network DCFC coverage appears thin in public directories. High elevation and winter weather raise range anxiety. AADT ~8,000–12,000. This is the clearest highway-gap thesis, but utility capacity and winter uptime must be proven before capital.

Gap score: 5/5 — over 100 mi with no reliable public DCFC option

CRITICAL — Battle Mountain to Wells, NV

~130 miles of Nevada high desert where non-Tesla DCFC coverage needs site-by-site confirmation. Tesla coverage may not equal open-network availability for all drivers. AADT ~10,000–15,000. Elko is the strongest amenity anchor inside this gap.

Gap score: 5/5 — Tesla-only coverage ≠ open-network coverage

HIGH — Rock Springs to Evanston, WY

~100 miles along the southwestern Wyoming approach to Utah. The position is compelling for last-stop / first-stop behavior near the state line, but the score depends on the exact power level, reliability, and access of existing stations in Evanston.

Gap score: 4/5 — existing sites are low-power or Tesla-first

WATCH — Ogallala to Sidney, NE (Panhandle)

~100 miles of western Nebraska where DCFC coverage is thinner than Lincoln/Omaha but less obviously empty than Wyoming. Ogallala has tourism and I-76 junction logic, yet the current score stays watchlist until charger reliability and NEVI award timing are checked.

Gap score: 3/5 — some stations exist, but none clustered at primary stop

Gap lengths: AFDC + PlugShare directory screen (June 2026); AADT: FHWA / state traffic count sources. All gaps assume 50 kW+ open-network DCFC only; Tesla-only sites require separate access verification.

The CRITICAL gaps (score 5) represent immediate first-mover opportunity before NEVI-funded national operators lock up the best real estate. HIGH gaps (score 4) are the next tier.

Why this site wins

Rock Springs sits at the center of Wyoming's longest I-80 gap. The city has a full travel plaza ecosystem (Holiday Inn, McDonald's, Pilot/TA). Every eastbound or westbound EV driver on I-80 faces a 100–175 mile gap from this point — making Rock Springs the only viable stop in a large radius.

NEVI-ready profile

Wyoming NEVI allocates planning funds through its state program. Rock Springs I-80 exit is on the designated AFC corridor. A 4× 150 kW DCFC installation should be tested against WYDOT's current RFP, utility-capacity rules, and eligible-cost definitions before assuming any award.

AADT: Wyoming DOT 2024 Traffic Count Summary (~8,000–12,000 at Rock Springs I-80 interchange). NEVI: WYDOT NEVI program page. Gap: AFDC locator June 2026.

Rock Springs is a pure highway DCFC play. Low local EV density means revenue depends heavily on highway traffic capture. At 3% highway EV share (2025 EV penetration trending), 8–12k AADT generates ~24–36 potential EV sessions/day passing the site before capture rate. Assumes 15–25% of passers stop.

Why this site wins

Elko is Nevada's only major city on I-80 east of Winnemucca. It has full-service lodging (Holiday Inn Express, Best Western, casinos), fast food clusters, and established travel plaza infrastructure. EV drivers traveling Salt Lake City → Reno need exactly one reliable stop: Elko. Mining industry fleet adoption is an emerging secondary market.

Nevada NEVI context

Nevada NEVI allocates ~$36M. NDOT has designated the full I-80 corridor. Elko's hotel cluster means dwell time is high — EV travelers who need 45–60 minutes to charge can eat or sleep, making L2 + DCFC mix viable even at lower local EV density.

AADT: Nevada DOT traffic count (~12,000–18,000 at Elko). NEVI: NDOT Nevada EV Infrastructure Program. Amenity: Google Maps POI data, TripAdvisor hotel listings (2026).

Elko wins on amenity quality more than raw traffic. The casino-hotel cluster enables dwell-time charging and L2 revenue that a bare highway pull-off cannot. Secondary angle: mining fleet operators (Barrick, Nevada Copper) may negotiate fleet contracts as adoption grows.

Why Lincoln is different

Lincoln is the only corridor site with meaningful local recurring demand (1,200–1,500 estimated resident EVs). The Fallbrook candidate site sits adjacent to ~1,500 Class A apartment units that have no dedicated home L2 charging. This enables a membership model that pure highway DCFC sites cannot access. I-80 AADT of 30–45k through Lincoln is the highest of any underpenetrated-DCFC site on the corridor.

Full diligence underway

Active site analysis: 8×L2 + 2×DCFC mixed-use concept. Nebraska's corridor plan calls for minimum four-charger DCFC sites in phase one, so the current Lincoln concept should be treated as a local-demand station unless redesigned with 4× DCFC or specifically cleared by NDOT.

The University of Iowa has sustainability goals and a growing EV fleet. A co-located charging station near US-218 / I-80 could serve campus fleet, student parking, and I-80 travelers simultaneously. Iowa NEVI allocates ~$32M; Iowa DOT is in active NEVI site selection (2026).

Net cost after NEVI (gap-play site)

Gross: ~$1.5–2M. If a state awards the maximum federal share, eligible costs could be reimbursed up to 80%, but land, some site work, contingency, and working capital may still sit outside the grant. Net owner outlay is therefore site- and award-specific.

NEVI is reimbursement, not upfront cash — owner must have construction financing. Most NEVI awardees use SBA 7(a) or conventional construction loans with grant as backstop.

§30C tax credit is no longer base-case

IRS guidance shows business charging property must be placed in service by June 30, 2026 to qualify under current rules. A new I-80 site screened in June 2026 is unlikely to permit, construct, energize, and place in service before that deadline.

Base case should exclude §30C. Any exception requires tax counsel, site-specific census-tract review, and proof the placed-in-service deadline is realistic.

NEVI standards: FHWA final rule (23 CFR 680). Nebraska program: NDOT NEVI page. §30C deadline and business credit amount: IRS Alternative Fuel Vehicle Refueling Property Credit page. All financing structures require legal, tax, and lender review.

CRITICAL: NEVI does not make a weak site investable and should not be shown as guaranteed. The owner must have a construction facility covering full gross cost; grants are competitive and reimbursement timing varies by state. §30C has been removed from base-case planning because the deadline is too near for new corridor development.
