Cumulative cash position by year (revised 16 L2 + 3 DCFC build, $953K gross capex, flat $170K/yr post-ramp EBITDA). Without grants or terminal value, the project pays back its capital around Year 8–9. With a host-pad contribution, recovery moves toward Year 6–7. This is normal for charging infrastructure — public DCFC networks typically model 7–12-year payback.

This slide reframes the "5-year cash after capex = -$520k" line that has been read as "do not proceed." The correct frame is that EV charging is an 8–10 year infrastructure asset that turns operating-cash positive in Year 2 and recovers capex around Year 8 under base assumptions. Owners requiring 5-year full payback should structure the deal around host contributions or do not proceed.

08

INCENTIVES

Do not count Section 30C
in the base case.

The deadline is real

The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (signed July 2025) accelerated §30C termination to property placed in service on or before June 30, 2026 — 47 days from this deck.

~6–9 morealistic permit → build → commission timeline for a 19-stall station
$0§30C credit value assumed in the capital plan

Counterfactual + substitutes

At 30% with prevailing-wage compliance on ~$700K of eligible equipment, the credit would have been ~$210K — material loss. Substitute paths:

  • Retail-host capital contribution (target: $100–200K)
  • Nebraska Energy Office state grant ($25–50K typical)
  • LES load-management rebate (utility-side rather than federal)
  • Apartment-developer co-investment in exchange for resident reservations

Sources: IRS §30C overview, H.R.1 (OBBBA) text. Owner action: confirm §30C status with a CPA before any external deck use; this is the most time-sensitive macro variable in the plan.

§30C is effectively unavailable for any new charging-station project starting in May 2026. The deck must drop §30C from the capital stack entirely — otherwise an investor or lender will read it as upside and inflate the implied stack.
