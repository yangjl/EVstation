THE GAP

Nebraska's EV infrastructure
is behind the curve.

Highway desert

I-80 is the nation's busiest east-west corridor — yet Lincoln has only a handful of public DCFC stalls, and most are tied to a single retailer.

No home charging

High-end apartment residents bought EVs but can't install a wall charger. They drive to a fast charger nightly — and resent it.

Federal money waiting

NEVI dollars are allocated to Nebraska, but private operators in Lincoln have been slow to claim them.

Cite AFDC station map + Nebraska NEVI allocation. Frame as solvable gap, not market failure. Slide 2 in PITCH_DECK_PLAN.md.

03

CUSTOMERS

Two overlapping segments.

I-80 travelers

Out-of-state EV drivers crossing Nebraska. Need: 20-minute DCFC top-up + clean restroom + safe lighting. Price-insensitive when stranded.

Class A apartment residents

EV owners in Wilderness Hills / Fallbrook / South 27th. No garage. Need: overnight L2 within 5 min of home. Will pay membership pass for guaranteed access.

Local commuters (overflow)

Lincoln EV drivers without home charging. Use the site as a backup. Stickiness through the membership program.

Why dual-positioning works

DCFC peaks at midday (travelers). L2 peaks 7pm – 8am (residents). Same canopy, same site, complementary utilization curves.

Customer hypotheses — NOT validated yet. Discovery work is item #1 on the next-steps list. Cite CUSTOMER_DISCOVERY.md.

Network operator, not site owner. We can co-list on their platform without ceding margin.
