CUSTOMERS BY THE NUMBERS

Start with the customers
who can repeat.

Apartment residents

Primary recurring segment. Planning catchment: ~1,500 apartment units within roughly one mile of Fallbrook/Tallgrass. At 1-3% EV penetration, that implies 21-63 current EV households before future adoption.

I-80 travelers

Higher-ticket DCFC sessions. Planning range: 150-450 EVs/day through the corridor using 30-45k AADT and 0.5-1.0% EV share; only a small fraction needs to stop to matter.

Local traffic

Nearby north Lincoln arterials show meaningful daily flow: N 27th near I-80 ramps ranges from ~9k to ~33k ADT in the 2024 City traffic list, depending on segment.

Load-shape logic

DCFC peaks during daytime travel windows. L2 demand should concentrate after work and overnight. The site must prove both patterns with actual usage or pre-launch commitments.

Sources: City of Lincoln 2024 ADT list; NDOT AADT viewer; apartment listings; PlugShare/ChargeHub/AFDC. All customer conversion rates remain human-review assumptions.

Adds actual customer sizing formulas. Survey 20-30 apartment residents and interview 3-5 property managers before using membership conversion externally.

COMPETITION

National chains. Walled gardens.
We're local and open.

Tesla Supercharger

Closed network until recently. Limited L2 footprint. We serve every car, including the Teslas.

Electrify America

DCFC-only. No overnight L2 for residents. Reliability complaints in PlugShare reviews.

ChargePoint

Network operator, not site owner. We can co-list on their platform without ceding margin.

Volt & Go

Locally-owned. Open standards. Dual L2 + DCFC. Apartment partnerships. Visible community accountability.

Cite COMPETITIVE_LANDSCAPE.md. Differentiation is positioning + ownership model, not technology. Don't oversell the moat.
