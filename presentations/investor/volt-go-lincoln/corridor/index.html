THE MANDATE + THE GAP

$5 billion says build now.
Most miles are still dark.

11states I-80 traverses, each with separate NEVI allocation and RFP schedule

NEVI forces infrastructure investment

Each state must deploy NEVI stations on Alternative Fuel Corridors — I-80 is fully designated. Stations require 4× ≥150 kW DCFC, 600 kW simultaneous capacity, 97% uptime, and location within 1 mile of an I-80 ramp. Private operators win NEVI awards by meeting these specs.

Source: FHWA NEVI final rule (2023); individual state NEVI plans.

Early movers capture the best sites

NEVI awards are competitive per-state RFPs. High-traffic sites with existing amenity anchors (travel plazas, fast food, lodging) draw the most sessions and command first-mover advantage before national chains (TA, Pilot, Sheetz) lock up premium parcels. Several states are in second-round RFPs in 2026.

Key thesis: the highest-scoring sites are where gap + amenity quality + grant timing all align — not just where traffic is highest.

SCORING FRAMEWORK

Five factors.
One composite score.

01 Traffic density — AADT on the I-80 segment near the site. Higher traffic = more potential sessions. Scored 1 (under 5k/day) to 5 (over 40k/day).
02 DCFC gap — miles to the nearest open-network DC fast charger (50 kW+). Scored 1 (under 30 mi) to 5 (over 100 mi). Gaps create captive demand.
03 Local EV density — resident EV registrations within a 5-mile catchment. Drives non-highway utilization and membership revenue. Scored 1–5 by county EV penetration estimates.
04 Amenity anchor — quality of co-located services: lodging, full-service food, restrooms, retail. EV drivers dwell longer at quality stops. Scored 1 (bare highway pull-off) to 5 (full-service travel plaza).
05 Grant eligibility — NEVI corridor status, IRA §30C tax credit eligibility (income census tract), and state co-funding availability. Scored 1 (ineligible) to 5 (NEVI corridor + §30C + state match).
/25 Maximum composite score is 25. Sites scoring 17–25 are priority candidates. Sites below 13 are passed for this cycle.

Scores are preliminary estimates based on publicly available traffic, EV registration, NEVI program, and amenity data. Human review required before using scores to commit capital.

The scoring model is a screening tool, not a financial projection. Each factor warrants independent diligence before site commitment.

THE CRITICAL GAPS

Five stretches where
EVs run out of options.

CRITICAL — Laramie to Rock Springs, WY

~175 miles of I-80 with minimal open-network DCFC coverage. High elevation (up to 8,640 ft at Elk Mountain) reduces EV range by 15–25% in cold months. AADT ~8,000–12,000. Closest reliable DCFC: Laramie (~2 stalls) or Rock Springs. Only Wyoming stop for trans-continental EV drivers.

Gap score: 5/5 — over 100 mi with no reliable public DCFC option

CRITICAL — Battle Mountain to Wells, NV

~130 miles of Nevada high desert with no confirmed open-network DCFC (as of mid-2026). Winnemucca has a Tesla Supercharger but limited Magic Dock availability. AADT ~10,000–15,000. Elevation and heat both degrade range seasonally.

Gap score: 5/5 — Tesla-only coverage ≠ open-network coverage

HIGH — Rock Springs to Evanston, WY

~100 miles along the southwestern Wyoming approach to Utah. One of the most heavily trucked I-80 sections west of Denver. AADT ~10,000–16,000. Evanston is the last Wyoming city before the Utah state line — strong captive demand position.

Gap score: 4/5 — existing sites are low-power or Tesla-first

HIGH — Ogallala to Sidney, NE (Panhandle)

~100 miles of western Nebraska with sparse DCFC coverage. Ogallala is the key service stop (Lake McConaughy tourism + I-80 crossing). I-76 junction at Big Springs adds Colorado-bound traffic. AADT ~12,000–17,000.

Gap score: 3/5 — some stations exist, but none clustered at primary stop

Gap lengths: AFDC + PlugShare directory (June 2026); AADT: FHWA TMS state counts. All gaps assume 50 kW+ open-network DCFC only; Tesla-only sites counted separately.

The CRITICAL gaps (score 5) represent immediate first-mover opportunity before NEVI-funded national operators lock up the best real estate. HIGH gaps (score 4) are the next tier.

Why this site wins

Rock Springs sits at the center of Wyoming's longest I-80 gap. The city has a full travel plaza ecosystem (Holiday Inn, McDonald's, Pilot/TA). Every eastbound or westbound EV driver on I-80 faces a 100–175 mile gap from this point — making Rock Springs the only viable stop in a large radius.

NEVI-ready profile

Wyoming NEVI allocates ~$25M. Rock Springs I-80 exit is on the designated AFC corridor. A 4× 150 kW DCFC installation would meet NEVI specs and qualify for 75–80% reimbursement on eligible equipment + installation costs. Wyoming DOT NEVI RFP is in Round 2 (2026).

AADT: Wyoming DOT 2024 Traffic Count Summary (~8,000–12,000 at Rock Springs I-80 interchange). NEVI: WYDOT NEVI program page. Gap: AFDC locator June 2026.

Rock Springs is a pure highway DCFC play. Low local EV density means revenue depends heavily on highway traffic capture. At 3% highway EV share (2025 EV penetration trending), 8–12k AADT generates ~24–36 potential EV sessions/day passing the site before capture rate. Assumes 15–25% of passers stop.

Why this site wins

Elko is Nevada's only major city on I-80 east of Winnemucca. It has full-service lodging (Holiday Inn Express, Best Western, casinos), fast food clusters, and established travel plaza infrastructure. EV drivers traveling Salt Lake City → Reno need exactly one reliable stop: Elko. Mining industry fleet adoption is an emerging secondary market.

Nevada NEVI context

Nevada NEVI allocates ~$36M. NDOT has designated the full I-80 corridor. Elko's hotel cluster means dwell time is high — EV travelers who need 45–60 minutes to charge can eat or sleep, making L2 + DCFC mix viable even at lower local EV density.

AADT: Nevada DOT traffic count (~12,000–18,000 at Elko). NEVI: NDOT Nevada EV Infrastructure Program. Amenity: Google Maps POI data, TripAdvisor hotel listings (2026).

Elko wins on amenity quality more than raw traffic. The casino-hotel cluster enables dwell-time charging and L2 revenue that a bare highway pull-off cannot. Secondary angle: mining fleet operators (Barrick, Nevada Copper) may negotiate fleet contracts as adoption grows.

Net cost after NEVI (gap-play site)

Gross: ~$1.5–2M. NEVI-eligible equipment + install ~$800k–1.2M at 75–80% reimbursement = ~$640k–960k grant. Net owner outlay: $400k–800k. Site work, land, contingency, and working capital not NEVI-eligible.

NEVI is reimbursement, not upfront cash — owner must have construction financing. Most NEVI awardees use SBA 7(a) or conventional construction loans with grant as backstop.

IRA §30C tax credit (all sites)

30% of qualified equipment cost (up to $100,000 per charger) for charging property placed in service after 2022. Requires location in eligible census tract (low-income or rural) and prevailing-wage compliance for full 30% rate. Wyoming + rural Nebraska sites likely qualify.

Stack: NEVI reimbursement + §30C credit + SBA 7(a) loan = lowest effective net capex. Do not assume stacking without tax counsel review.

NEVI reimbursement rates: FHWA NEVI final rule (23 CFR 680). IRA §30C: IRS Notice 2023-29, Rev. Proc. 2023-32. SBA 7(a): SBA.gov. All financing structures require legal, tax, and lender review.

CRITICAL: NEVI does not fund site work, land, or contingency. The owner must have a construction facility covering full gross cost; NEVI reimburses after milestones. Do not project NEVI as certain — many state RFP awards are competitive and undersubscribed applicants lose to national operators.
