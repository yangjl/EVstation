# Market Research — Volt & Go Lincoln EV Charging Station

## Purpose

Track market evidence, sources, sizing logic, trends, and open gaps for the Lincoln, NE EV charging station business plan.

## Market Definition

- **Target market:** Public EV charging services in Lincoln, Nebraska
- **Customer segments:**
  1. Highway travelers on Interstate 80 (east-west transcontinental corridor)
  2. EV-owning residents of high-end apartments lacking dedicated home charging
  3. Lincoln commuters and city drivers seeking reliable public charging
- **Geography:** Lincoln, NE MSA; primary draw radius ~5 miles from station; I-80 travelers passing through
- **Time horizon:** 5-year plan (2026–2031)
- **Included categories:** Level 2 AC charging, DC Fast Charging (DCFC), charging network subscription revenue
- **Excluded categories:** Tesla-proprietary Supercharger business model, fleet depot charging, residential home charger installation

## Market Context — Lincoln, Nebraska

### City Overview
- Population: ~295,000 (Lincoln MSA ~340,000); Nebraska's capital and home of the University of Nebraska
- Income: Median household income ~$62,000; significant high-income professional population
- Growth: One of the faster-growing mid-size cities in the Midwest
- I-80: Lincoln sits directly on I-80, the primary east-west interstate connecting Chicago to San Francisco; heavy truck and passenger car traffic year-round

### EV Adoption
- Nebraska EV registrations have been growing ~35–45% year-over-year nationally; Nebraska tracks slightly below national average but acceleration is visible
- Lincoln and Omaha lead Nebraska EV adoption; college-educated, higher-income households drive uptake
- As of 2025, Nebraska has ~8,000–12,000 registered EVs; Lincoln likely holds 2,000–4,000 of those
- High-end apartment residents are more likely to own EVs and lack dedicated Level 2 home charging — a structural gap this station addresses

### Charging Infrastructure Gap
- Nebraska is part of the NEVI (National Electric Vehicle Infrastructure) program, which funds charging corridors along I-80; significant federal investment is flowing into the state
- Lincoln's existing public charging is concentrated downtown and near the university; few high-quality stations near I-80 exit ramps combined with residential density
- The station's dual positioning (highway-adjacent + apartment-adjacent) targets two underserved use cases simultaneously

### Comparable Market Benchmarks
- Industry average DC Fast Charger utilization at mature stations: 20–35%
- Industry average Level 2 charger utilization: 10–25%
- Average EV charging session: Level 2 ~45–60 min delivering ~7–10 kWh; DCFC ~20–35 min delivering ~25–40 kWh
- Typical charging price: Level 2 ~$0.35–0.40/kWh; DCFC ~$0.45–0.55/kWh in comparable mid-size markets
