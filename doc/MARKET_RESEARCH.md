# Market Research — Volt & Go Lincoln EV Charging Station

## Purpose

Track market evidence, sources, sizing logic, trends, and open gaps for the Lincoln, NE EV charging station business plan.

## Market Definition

- **Target market:** Public EV charging services in Lincoln, Nebraska
- **Customer segments:**
  1. Highway travelers on Interstate 80 (east-west transcontinental corridor)
  2. EV-owning residents of high-end apartments lacking dedicated home charging
  3. Lincoln commuters and city drivers seeking reliable public charging
- **Geography:** Lincoln, NE MSA; primary draw radius ~5 miles from station; I-80 travelers passing through
- **Time horizon:** 5-year plan (2026–2031)
- **Included categories:** Level 2 AC charging, DC Fast Charging (DCFC), charging network subscription revenue
- **Excluded categories:** Tesla-proprietary Supercharger business model, fleet depot charging, residential home charger installation

## Market Context — Lincoln, Nebraska

### City Overview
- Population: ~295,000 (Lincoln MSA ~340,000); Nebraska's capital and home of the University of Nebraska
- Income: Median household income ~$62,000; significant high-income professional population
- Growth: One of the faster-growing mid-size cities in the Midwest
- I-80: Lincoln sits directly on I-80, the primary east-west interstate connecting Chicago to San Francisco; heavy truck and passenger car traffic year-round

### EV Adoption — confirmed numbers (v0.2, 2026-05-13)
- **Nebraska:** ~9,490 registered EVs statewide as of June 2025 (Nebraska Department of Water, Energy, and Environment).
- **Lincoln (Lancaster County):** No published county-level count located. Lincoln is ~16% of Nebraska's population, suggesting **~1,200–1,500 EVs** as a planning estimate (DRAFT — confirm with Lancaster County Treasurer's office).
- **Growth signal:** U.S. national EV share of new car sales held at 9% in 2024 (down slightly from 10% in 2023); the Nebraska absolute base is small but growing fast off a low base.
- High-end apartment residents are more likely to own EVs and lack dedicated Level 2 home charging — a structural gap this station addresses.

### I-80 Traffic — Lincoln corridor
- Lincoln sits on I-80 with 5 interchanges in the city: Exit 397 (US-77 S), Exit 399 (NW 12th / Cornhusker Hwy), Exit 401 (I-180 / US-34), Exit 403 (27th St), Exit 405 (US-77 N).
- **AADT (Annual Average Daily Traffic):** NDOT's 2024 annual count report covers I-80 through Lincoln; published segment values typically fall in the **30,000–45,000 vehicles/day** range. Exact mile-marker AADT must be pulled from NDOT's AADT viewer (`nebraskamap.gov/datasets/annual-average-daily-traffic-counts`) before quoting.
- Lincoln rest area is at mile marker ~397 (westbound) / 398 (eastbound).
- NDOT publishes traffic counts on a 12 or 24 Hour Intersection Turning Movement basis annually; Lincoln also maintains its own ADT list at `lincoln.ne.gov/.../adtv/2024-list.pdf`.

### Charging Infrastructure Gap — confirmed (v0.2, 2026-05-13)
- **Lincoln has ~155 total public charging ports across ~32 sites** (PlugShare directory, May 2026).
- **Of those, only ~18 are DCFC.** 16 are Tesla (Hy-Vee #3 at 5020 N 27th and West O at 610 W O St — 8 stalls each); the remaining ~2–4 ports are Electrify America at Casey's (110 NW 20th).
- **Non-Tesla DCFC sites in all of Lincoln: 1.** This is the central structural gap the candidate site exploits.
- **City of Lincoln received a $640,000 USDOT grant in January 2025 to install ~20 new public ports** targeting low/moderate-income areas — this is competitive supply but largely targets a different submarket than Class A apartments.
- Lincoln's existing public charging is concentrated downtown (LES Haymarket Green, city parking garages, UNL campus) and near retail anchors (Hy-Vee, Russ's Market, Super Saver).
- The station's dual positioning (highway-adjacent + apartment-adjacent) plus the open-network DCFC angle targets three underserved use cases simultaneously.

### Class A Apartment Cluster — Fallbrook / North Lincoln
- **Brookside Apartments at Fallbrook** — 7300 Tallgrass Pkwy, Lincoln NE 68521. Studio/1/2-BR Class A; stainless appliances, quartz, in-unit W/D, resort pool. Starting rent ~$959/mo. ~300 units (est.).
- **Northbrook Apartments** — 27th & Fletcher, Lincoln NE. 1/2/3-BR, pet-friendly.
- **Fallbrook (master development)** — 570 Fallbrook Blvd & Stonebrook Pkwy. Mixed-use, ongoing construction.
- **Total Class A units within 1 mile of candidate site:** ~1,200–1,500 (DRAFT — verify with CoStar pull and direct property-manager outreach).
- Existing retail anchors on Fallbrook Blvd (Super Saver, Russ's Market) co-locate L2 retail chargers — confirms EV demand exists in the neighborhood but no overnight L2 product for residents.

### Comparable Market Benchmarks
- Industry average DC Fast Charger utilization at mature stations: 20–35%
- Industry average Level 2 charger utilization: 10–25%
- Average EV charging session: Level 2 ~45–60 min delivering ~7–10 kWh; DCFC ~20–35 min delivering ~25–40 kWh
- Typical charging price: Level 2 ~$0.35–0.40/kWh; DCFC ~$0.45–0.55/kWh in comparable mid-size markets

## I-80 Corridor Screen Addendum (v0.5.1 — 2026-06-13)

The I-80 corridor analysis now treats the 5-factor score as a **diligence-priority screen**, not a capital recommendation or return forecast. The scoring dimensions remain traffic exposure, DCFC gap, local EV density, amenity anchor, and grant / corridor-program fit.

**Current interpretation:** Rock Springs, WY and Elko, NV should be the first two new corridor-gap diligence targets. Lincoln remains the active mixed-model benchmark because it has local recurring demand and an existing site-strategy deck. Iowa City, IA and Evanston, WY stay on the watchlist unless a university / hospital partner, travel-plaza partner, parcel-control path, or active state funding window changes the risk profile.

**Capital-stack note:** §30C is excluded from base-case corridor planning for new sites because the current placed-in-service deadline is June 30, 2026. Any exception requires tax counsel and proof that the site can realistically be placed in service before the deadline.

**Required checks before using the corridor screen externally:**

1. Verify open-network DCFC gaps in AFDC, PlugShare, Tesla/NACS access status, and field calls.
2. Pull state DOT AADT counts for the exact interchange, not only the corridor segment.
3. Request utility demand-charge and service-upgrade guidance for each candidate exit.
4. Confirm the live NEVI / state RFP status and eligible-cost rules with WYDOT, Nevada DOT, and Nebraska DOT.
5. Get site-control terms before advancing any parcel as more than a screen.

## Candidate Site (v0.2 recommendation — DRAFT, owner must validate)

- **Submarket:** Fallbrook neighborhood, north Lincoln.
- **Approximate location:** ~7300 Tallgrass Pkwy / Fallbrook Blvd corridor (40.881°N, -96.728°W).
- **Distance to I-80 Exit 401 (I-180 / US-34):** ~1.5 mi (~4 minutes by car).
- **Distance to nearest competitor DCFC:**
  - Tesla SC at 5020 N 27th — ~2.6 mi east (Tesla-first network).
  - Electrify America at 110 NW 20th — ~3.5 mi southwest (only open-network DCFC in Lincoln today).
- **Adjacent demand anchors:** Brookside Apartments at Fallbrook (next door), Northbrook (~1 mi east), Fallbrook master-planned development (mixed-use, expanding), Super Saver + Russ's Market retail co-tenancy on Fallbrook Blvd.
- **Why this site wins:** First-mover lock on the only Class A apartment cluster in Lincoln without a non-Tesla DCFC within 3 miles, plus direct I-80 highway access via Exit 401 / US-34.
- **Open questions for site selection:**
  1. Exact available parcels along Fallbrook Blvd / Tallgrass Pkwy and lease rates.
  2. Pad ownership vs. ground-lease vs. apartment-developer co-investment.
  3. Distance to LES transformer with capacity for 2 × 100 kW DCFC + 8 × 7.2 kW L2 simultaneous load.
