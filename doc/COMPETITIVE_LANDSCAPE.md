# Competitive Landscape — Volt & Go Lincoln EV Charging Station

## Purpose

Track direct competitors, substitutes, customer alternatives, differentiation, and competitive risks for the Lincoln, NE EV charging station.

## Competitor Register

| Competitor or alternative | Category | Customer served | Strength | Weakness | Differentiation opportunity |
|---------------------------|----------|-----------------|----------|----------|-----------------------------|
| Electrify America (I-80 Nebraska) | Direct — DCFC | Highway travelers | National brand, high-power (150–350kW), CCS/CHAdeMO | Located at specific I-80 truck stops; may not be near residential; stations can be full during peak travel | Choose location with closer proximity to high-density apartments; add quality amenities |
| ChargePoint network (Lincoln) | Direct — L2/DCFC | Local residents, commuters | Large national network, strong app ecosystem, good reliability | Often installed in parking garages, malls; limited highway draw; lower power than DCFC | Physical convenience closer to apartments; competitive pricing; superior site amenities |
| Tesla Supercharger (Lincoln) | Partial direct — DCFC | Tesla owners only | Very reliable, fast, proprietary Tesla UX | Tesla-only access (though opening to non-Tesla EVs in 2023–2025) | Serve all EV brands (CCS/CHAdeMO open standards); non-Tesla EV owners represent growing majority |
| Blink Charging (Lincoln area) | Direct — L2 | Local commuters | Installed in some parking lots and hotels | Historically lower reliability scores; older hardware at some locations | Newer, more reliable hardware; better customer UX; loyalty program |
| Home charging (Level 1/Level 2) | Substitute — L1/L2 | EV owners with home access | Lowest cost per kWh; most convenient for owners with garages | Unavailable to apartment dwellers without dedicated EVSE; slow (overnight) | Directly serves apartment residents who cannot install home chargers — this is core use case |
| Dealership / destination chargers | Indirect substitute | Customers with dealership access | Free in some cases | Limited availability, business hours only, not publicly accessible in all cases | 24/7 public access; no purchase requirement |
| Gas stations (ICE vehicles) | Deep substitute | Non-EV drivers | Universal infrastructure, fast refuel | Irrelevant as EV adoption grows; declining relevance | Not applicable yet; but site should be designed to accommodate future EV-only site |

## Positioning Notes

- **Current positioning hypothesis:** "The only premium, mixed-speed public charging station in Lincoln's southwest/I-80 corridor that serves both highway travelers and high-end apartment residents — open 24/7, all EV brands welcome."
- **Evidence supporting the position:** Limited DCFC coverage near I-80 interchanges adjacent to high-density residential in Lincoln (needs local verification); apartment residents structurally underserved by home charging.
- **Risks or weak claims:**
  - Electrify America may open new stations along I-80 within 2–3 years
  - Tesla Supercharger opening to all brands reduces differentiation gap
  - ChargePoint may expand into the same corridor
  - Low initial brand recognition vs. national chains
- **Next validation step:** Use AFDC station locator + Google Maps to map all existing and planned public chargers within 3 miles of target I-80 interchanges in Lincoln; interview apartment property managers to understand EV parking demand and competitor presence.

## Differentiation Strategy

1. **Location:** Dual-access positioning (highway off-ramp + visible from high-end apartment community) is the primary moat
2. **Open standards:** Serve all EV brands (CCS, CHAdeMO, J1772) — no proprietary lock-in
3. **Amenities:** Covered canopy, lighting, Wi-Fi, clean site — better than bare-lot competitors
4. **Membership pass:** Monthly subscription for apartment residents to drive loyalty and predictable revenue
5. **Local ownership:** Community-invested owner vs. national chain; potential for faster issue resolution and local partnerships
