## Volt & Go Lincoln EV Charging Station

## 1. Executive Conclusion

The proposed Lincoln, Nebraska EV charging station is an interesting concept, but the current plan should be revised from "ready-to-fund station plan" to "site-validation and incentive-eligibility plan." The business case is strongest if the owner can secure a site that combines three conditions:

1. A low-cost or partnership-based land structure near I-80.
2. Direct access to a Class A apartment or mixed-use customer base that lacks home charging.
3. A utility rate structure that keeps demand charges from overwhelming early utilization.

The existing 10-port concept, with 8 Level 2 chargers and 2 DC fast chargers, is sensible for a mixed residential/highway site, but it may not match the requirements or expectations for NEVI-funded corridor charging. Nebraska's NEVI plan focuses on I-80 buildout, with first-phase sites described as roughly 30-35 DC fast charging sites and a minimum of four chargers per site. That means the project needs a strategic choice:

**Owner decision required:** Should the station remain a smaller mixed-use local station, or should it be redesigned as a NEVI-oriented corridor station with at least four DC fast charging ports?

Until that choice is made, grant revenue and incentive offsets should be treated as upside, not base-case capital.

## 3. Business Model Revision

Keep the dual-use thesis, but split it into two design alternatives:

| Option | Description | Best fit | Main tradeoff |
|--------|-------------|----------|---------------|
| Option A: Mixed-use local station | 8 L2 + 2 DCFC, focused on apartment residents plus some highway traffic | Lower capital, local customer loyalty, resident memberships | May be weaker for NEVI corridor funding and long-distance traveler expectations |
| Option B: Corridor-oriented station | 4+ DCFC plus a smaller number of L2 ports | Better alignment with NEVI/I-80 corridor strategy and highway travelers | Higher capital, greater utility complexity, higher demand-charge exposure |

**Recommendation:** Use Option A as the default only if a strong apartment partnership or low-cost land deal is available. Use Option B only if Nebraska DOT/NEVI feedback confirms the project can compete for corridor funding and the utility quote supports the load profile.

## 4. Market Revision

The market need is plausible, but the plan should use more careful language.

### Stronger claims

- Lincoln is a credible location because it sits on I-80 and has a mix of local residents, university-related traffic, state-government employment, and regional travelers.
- Apartment residents without assigned home charging are a real structural customer segment for public or semi-public Level 2 charging.
- Nebraska is actively planning EV corridor infrastructure, with I-80 as a central focus.

### Claims that need more evidence

- "Structurally undersupplied near I-80 and high-end apartments" should be verified with AFDC, PlugShare, Google Maps, and field visits for each candidate exit.
- Lincoln-specific EV ownership counts and growth rates need a primary source or state registration data.
- The station should not claim it is the "only" or "first" premium mixed-speed station until a competitor map is built.

### Revised market language

Use this language internally:

> Volt & Go Lincoln is a proposed public EV charging station serving two overlapping use cases: I-80 travelers who need reliable fast charging and apartment or local residents who lack convenient home Level 2 charging. The opportunity depends on selecting a site where highway access, residential charging need, utility capacity, and land economics overlap.

Avoid this language until validated:

> Public DC fast charging near convenient I-80 interchanges adjacent to Class A apartments is structurally undersupplied.

## 5. Customer Revision

The customer story is directionally sound, but it is still hypothesis-stage.

| Segment | Revised priority | Reason |
|---------|------------------|--------|
| Apartment residents without home charging | Highest | This is the differentiated segment and may support recurring usage or memberships. |
| I-80 EV travelers | High | Supports DCFC revenue, but location and app visibility matter heavily. |
| Local commuters | Medium | Useful supplemental demand, but more likely to have substitutes across town. |

Complete these checks before committing to a site:

1. Survey 20-30 residents at candidate apartment communities.
2. Interview 3-5 property managers about EV charging demand, partnership interest, and existing vendor relationships.
3. Map existing chargers within 3 miles of each candidate site.
4. Review PlugShare and Google reviews for reliability complaints at nearby stations.
5. Estimate walking distance and actual customer behavior from apartments to the site.

## 6. Financial Revision

The current base case is not financeable as written because EBITDA remains negative through Year 5 under the $4,500/month land lease assumption.

| Year | Revenue | OpEx | EBITDA |
|------|---------|------|--------|
| Y1 | $63,440 | $144,000 | ($80,560) |
| Y2 | $96,040 | $152,500 | ($56,460) |
| Y3 | $132,100 | $165,620 | ($33,520) |
| Y4 | $155,370 | $176,290 | ($20,920) |
| Y5 | $170,820 | $185,510 | ($14,690) |

The station is not primarily a revenue-growth problem. It is a fixed-cost and utilization-timing problem. The first two years are likely to be fragile unless the owner lowers land cost, increases utilization, secures incentives, or adds an anchor partner.

The diligence goal should be to prove one of the following:

- Land cost at or below $3,000/month.
- Land contributed or discounted by apartment developer, retail host, city, or landowner.
- Confirmed grant/tax-credit package that reduces capital at risk.
- Higher initial utilization through apartment memberships, fleet users, or co-location with a high-dwell-time business.
- Utility rate plan that limits demand-charge shock during ramp-up.

## 7. Incentive and Regulatory Revision

### Nebraska NEVI

Nebraska DOT states that Nebraska is receiving about $30 million in NEVI funds, with the 2025 plan focused on electrifying I-80 and other key routes. NDOT describes the first phase as roughly 30-35 DC fast charging sites along designated corridors, with each site having a minimum of four chargers.

**Implication:** The current 2-DCFC design should not assume NEVI eligibility. The owner should ask NDOT whether a 2-DCFC mixed-use site can qualify, or whether the project must be redesigned with 4+ DCFC ports.

### Section 30C tax credit

The IRS currently describes the business credit as 6% of eligible cost, or 30% if prevailing wage and apprenticeship requirements are met, with a maximum of $100,000 per item. The IRS also states that eligible property must be placed in service in an eligible census tract and, under current guidance, the credit is available for qualifying property placed in service through June 30, 2026.

**Implication:** The plan should not simply model "$30,000-$100,000" as available. The selected parcel's census tract, placed-in-service timing, business structure, and labor compliance need confirmation.

### Utility rates

LES business-rate information confirms that commercial/industrial customers are assigned based on projected usage and demand, and LES recommends contacting staff to confirm rate application. For a charging site with DCFC load, this is a critical gating item.

**Implication:** The owner should request a written LES service and rate estimate for each candidate site before accepting the financial model.

## 8. Competitive Revision

The competitive strategy should shift from "open-standard station" to "reliable, mixed-use, future-compatible station."

The current plan mentions CCS, CHAdeMO, and J1772. That should be updated after vendor quotes because J3400/NACS is now being standardized across the market. The Department of Energy describes J1772, CHAdeMO, and J3400/NACS as connector types drivers may encounter, with J3400 being standardized for broader use.

**Recommendation:** Specify that the station should be CCS and J3400/NACS-ready for DCFC, with J1772 or J3400-compatible Level 2 depending on equipment availability. Treat CHAdeMO as optional unless local vehicle data supports it.

The strongest defensible differentiation is:

- reliable uptime
- safe lighting and cameras
- easy app/payment experience
- highway visibility
- apartment partnerships
- weather-aware site design
- pricing that is transparent and locally competitive

## 9. Revised Go/No-Go Criteria

Move forward only if at least four of the following five conditions are met:

| Criterion | Target |
|-----------|--------|
| Land | Lease at or below about $3,000/month, owned land, or partner-contributed site |
| Utility | LES confirms manageable rate schedule, demand charge exposure, and interconnect cost |
| Demand | Apartment/customer discovery shows credible recurring use |
| Incentives | NEVI/state/30C eligibility is confirmed for the exact parcel and design |
| Competition | No superior nearby station is planned or already serving the same use case |

If fewer than four are met, pause the project or redesign it around a different site/partner.
